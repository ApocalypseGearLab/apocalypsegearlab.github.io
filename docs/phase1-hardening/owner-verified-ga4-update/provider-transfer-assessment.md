## Basis, scope and proportionality

The operator/location/role and GA4 settings are owner-confirmed. The [fact record](evidence/owner-verified-facts.json) distinguishes the latest confirmations from remaining unknowns; it supersedes the earlier acceptance/BigQuery/access uncertainty without changing historical evidence. Source and passing browser evidence establish static hosting, browser-only preferences, tag gating, native retailer links and the existing catalogue search form. Visitor/request volumes are not measured here; field types and collection paths are known, actual record counts are not invented. There is no site checkout, account-registration system or privacy-upload form in the reviewed implementation. Google and GitHub may independently hold service/security records.

This is a bounded technology-project and outside-Quebec review, not a claim that every external link is an outsourced processor. The conservative scope includes hosting and mailbox service arrangements while distinguishing providers’ own security/account processing. The analysis follows the CAI’s [proportionate EFVP approach](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/uploads/pdfs/CAI_GU_EFVP.pdf) and its [private-enterprise transfer guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/utilisation-communication-renseignements-personnels).

Risk ratings below are qualitative project judgments: low means limited likely harm under the stated controls, medium means meaningful exposure or unresolved control/contract evidence, and high means plausible sensitive disclosure or account compromise requiring mitigation. They are not measured probabilities or a statutory scoring system.

**Findings:** H1 public accidental disclosure: inherent high for private request files, reduced by keeping them out of the repository; this is a continuing operational control, not a provider promise. H2 unavoidable request/security metadata and international/legal access: residual low-to-medium for this static architecture; no account/applicant database is hosted. H3 applicable contract/role coverage: the published standard terms are the disclosed working basis, not proof of a private agreement; flag a different enterprise/custom agreement if one changes this flow, and determine DPA scope for any on-behalf processing. No newly negotiated DPA is automatically demanded.

**Preliminary disposition:** no evidence currently requires replacing Pages or buying a new plan. The remaining decision is whether the documented limited flow and applicable written terms adequately address the covered hosting arrangement. Do not equate GitHub’s own security purpose with an exemption for every hosting obligation, or assume a GDPR contractual role decides Quebec-law classification.

## Cross-border legal-regime and written-arrangement finding

US/global provider infrastructure exposes these flows to legal regimes different from Quebec’s, including compelled provider disclosure. No blanket country-adequacy finding, exact server list or immunity from government access is asserted. Consider provider confidentiality/purpose limits, access controls, incident cooperation and rights/deletion assistance together with sensitivity and limited purposes. For covered arrangements, determine whether the actual written terms address the assessment’s risks; existing electronically accepted terms may be relevant writing, but their label alone proves neither adequacy nor a requirement for a separately negotiated document.

The service-contract exception for disclosures without consent and the outside-Quebec transfer requirements have different scopes; do not treat consent or a GDPR DPA as a substitute for the required Quebec assessment/arrangements. See the [requirements register](../release-blocker-resolution/release-requirements.md) for the precise distinctions.
